## Translated system error messages

`src/features/terminal/errorPhrases.generated.ts`, generated by `scripts/import-error-phrases.py`, holds short fixed parts of the error messages of the GNU C Library and GNU Bash, as their translators wrote them in the [Translation Project](https://translationproject.org) catalogues and in Ubuntu's language packs (Launchpad translations). They are used only to recognise error lines in terminal output when the system speaks another language. Thanks to those translation teams.

## ssh-key test vectors

`src-tauri/src/tools/keygen/testdata/` holds OpenSSH and PuTTYgen key files from the test examples of the [ssh-key](https://github.com/RustCrypto/SSH) crate (MIT OR Apache-2.0), used only by the tests to check PuTTY `.ppk` compatibility. They are not part of the application.


## AI command-line tool icons

The tab column of DEV mode shows the marks of Claude (Claude Code) and OpenAI (Codex) as drawn by [Simple Icons](https://simpleicons.org) 15.22.0 ([simple-icons/simple-icons](https://github.com/simple-icons/simple-icons)), whose icon data is released under [CC0 1.0](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/); the SVG paths are copied into `src/features/devmode/CliIcon.tsx`. The marks are trademarks of their owners (Anthropic and OpenAI), used only to name the tool a tab runs; PlexusTerm is not affiliated with or endorsed by them. Antigravity's mark is the arch of its official favicon (https://antigravity.google/favicon.svg, without its round background), copied into `src/features/devmode/antigravityMark.ts`; it belongs to Google. pi's mark is the three blocks of its official favicon (https://pi.dev/favicon.svg), drawn in the text colour in `src/features/devmode/CliIcon.tsx`; it belongs to the pi project. gentle-shell is shown with the rose of [gentle-ai](https://github.com/Gentleman-Programming/gentle-ai) (`docs/assets/brand/rose.png`, MIT License, © Gentleman Programming), traced to vector paths in `src/features/devmode/roseMark.ts`. These marks too are used only to name the tool a tab runs. OpenCode, with no mark used here, is shown as a plain letter badge drawn by PlexusTerm.
